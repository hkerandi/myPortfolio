02 — Featured project

F1 · Data visualisation · Games · XR

Data in Motion

Exploring how data can move beyond the dashboard and become something you can see, interact with and experience, built on a pipeline for real Formula 1 telemetry.

The real question

What does it take to turn data into a reliable, responsive, interactive experience?

The problem
Public F1 telemetry arrives uneven, with gaps, duplicate timestamps and even vehicles that aren't race cars. Every downstream decision, from a pit call to a replay, inherits those errors. The job is to catch them before they spread.
The system
Python and FastF1 extract each session and normalise every time column to plain seconds, so nothing downstream has to parse durations. Nim does the heavy lifting (ingestion, validation, resampling) because it's compiled and fast. Results land in PostgreSQL with TimescaleDB, a time-series database, running in Docker so anyone can reproduce the stack with one command. Flags, a desktop lap viewer and a WebXR ride-along all read from the same cleaned data.
The execution
The Nim lap viewer reads only the rows it needs, drops incomplete ones, sorts and de-duplicates timestamps, then interpolates between samples to replay any driver's fastest lap of the 2022 Italian Grand Prix at true speed. Laps with gaps over two seconds are flagged on screen: bad data is shown, not hidden. When F1 locked its public timing archive in October 2026, I switched sources, documented the change, and kept the project moving.
The evidence
The repository, with a log of every data problem found so far: a source lockout, non-race vehicles in the position feed, 89 "missing" laps explained by retirements, uneven sampling, and no heading data, each with how it's handled.
The next step
A data-quality report, resampling onto a distance grid, automatic flags, then Ride as Verstappen, see the data: a WebXR lap that reveals the raw samples, the cleaning and the flags on track.

03 — Digital experiences

Babe!

A privacy-first health diary designed around the realities of Black women's health experiences: a private, unhurried place to understand your body over time.

The problem
The small details of our health get lost between appointments, and too often our own experiences get talked over.
The system
A deliberate architecture choice: local-first. Everything lives on the device in SQLite, with no server, no account and no central store to breach. Privacy is a design constraint, not a setting. Your health data belongs to you.
The execution
A React Native app for journaling, cycle tracking, mood and body observations, symptoms, sick days and personal remedies.
The next step
Wider testing, and listening closely to the women using it.

React Native · Expo · JavaScript · SQLite · iOS / TestFlight · Android SDK

04 — Research

Research, with presence

Live · Full-stack geospatial platform

Mapping PA Prisons

The platform behind my carceral research: Pennsylvania's prison system, mapped from 50K+ incarceration records.

Data
Six monthly datasets integrating population, geographic and security-level data.
Pipeline
I designed and maintain the ETL pipeline. I diagnosed schema drift, type mismatches, phantom columns and whitespace errors, then built ingestion validation that checks each monthly dataset independently, so problems are caught before they reach the app.
Delivery
Cleaned data flows to the application automatically, with month-specific data lazy-loaded for speed.

PostgreSQL · Python · Pandas · ArcGIS · React · Git

Carceral Algorithms: How Misconducts Affect Parole Outcomes in Pennsylvania

Question
How do misconducts factor into parole outcomes?
Method
An end-to-end Python pipeline over 10,000+ Pennsylvania Department of Corrections misconduct records, with reproducible cleaning, validation and analysis.
Finding
Treating misconducts as a single factor hides most of the story; their types, patterns and relationships tell it.

1st Place · Best in Show, data challenge

Cultural Diffusion in Global Music: The Billboard Hot 100

Question
What can 50+ years of Billboard Hot 100 data say about how popular music changes?
Method
A reproducible pipeline over 152K+ chart records, integrating linguistic, categorical and geographic data. I normalised decades of inconsistent historical data (type mismatches, whitespace, edge cases) and designed an analytical model for comparing eras.
Result
Lyrical themes and sentiment compared across decades; awarded 1st Place and Best in Show.

Python · Pandas · Jupyter
